PhD cold-email checklist

For emailing a potential PhD advisor (or supervisor) before or alongside your application. Print it, or keep it open while you write.

1. Pick the right people

  • They published on your topic in the last 2–3 years, not just once a decade ago.
  • They are often the last author (in most lab sciences and CS, that is the lab head). In maths, theory and economics, author order is usually alphabetical, so ignore this signal there.
  • Their papers have several different first authors: a sign of a lab with students.
  • Check their own page for "not taking students" notes, sabbaticals or a move to industry.
  • Early-career faculty (assistant professors, lecturers) are often actively recruiting and more likely to reply.

2. Before you write

  • Read one recent paper they led (first or last author), not a 60-author consortium paper.
  • Skip corrections, errata and editorials when choosing a paper to mention.
  • Check their title: in the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and Canada many academics are "Dr", not "Professor".
  • Check whether the department wants you to contact faculty before applying at all; some say not to.

3. The email

  • Subject: "Prospective PhD student (Fall 2027): your work on <short paper title>".
  • First two lines: who you are, your degree and university, the intake you are applying for, and the direct question: are they taking students?
  • One specific sentence about their paper: the idea or result that stood out, and why. This is the sentence that gets replies.
  • One or two sentences on your most relevant project and its result, with a number if you have one.
  • CV attached as a PDF named Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf. No long transcripts in the body.
  • Under 150 words. No "I am writing to express my interest". No copy-pasted paragraphs about their whole career.
  • Fill every [bracket]. Read it once aloud. Check the name spelling twice.
Subject: Prospective PhD student (Fall 2027): your work on "<short title>"

Dear Prof. <Surname>,

I am <name>, a final-year <degree> student at <university>, applying for PhD admission for Fall 2027. Are you taking new PhD students in your group?

I read your 2026 paper "<title>". <One specific sentence on what stood out and why.>

<One or two sentences on your most relevant project and its result.>

My CV is attached. I would be grateful for a short reply, even if your group is full.

Best regards,
<name>

4. Sending and following up

  • Send Tuesday–Thursday morning in their time zone.
  • One professor per email. Never CC several professors.
  • No reply after 10–14 days? Send one short follow-up in the same thread. Then stop.
  • Keep a tracker: who, when you emailed, when to follow up, what they said.
  • Most US deadlines fall on Dec 1 and Dec 15. Email 4–8 weeks before, not the week of.

5. Red flags to avoid

  • Wrong name, wrong title, or a paper they did not write.
  • "Dear Sir/Madam" or "Respected Sir".
  • Attaching five files, or asking them to read your thesis.
  • Asking about funding before they have shown interest.
